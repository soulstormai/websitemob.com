Cut the
Middle Man.

The best tools to do almost everything yourself in October 2026, the must-haves, what they cost, and why they are worth it.

// BY WEBSITEMOB// OCTOBER 2026// 7 MIN READ

Every layer between you and your customer takes a cut: the marketplace that lists your product, the agency that builds your site, the studio that makes your video, the platform that holds your notes. In October 2026, AI tools have made most of those layers optional. This is the short list of what to use instead, what it costs, and why each one earns a place.

Prices below come from each vendor's pricing page or from price roundups checked between September 23 and October 8, 2026. They change often, so confirm at checkout.

1. Your own domain and free hosting

Own the address people use to find you. A domain is a yearly fee, and a simple static page can run on a free hosting plan, such as Cloudflare Pages or Vercel at the time of writing. That removes the website-builder subscription that sits between you and your visitors. It is the foundation for everything else here.

2. Sell direct instead of renting a storefront

Marketplaces bring traffic, but they also take fees and own the customer relationship. An own-store platform like Shopify lets you keep the customer list and the brand. Use a marketplace to get found, and send repeat buyers to your own store.

3. Build apps without hiring a developer

AI app builders turn a description into a working app. Base44 paid plans start at $16 a month billed annually. Lovable's Pro plan is $25 a month, and Bolt's Pro is also $25. If you write code, Cursor has a free tier and a Plus plan at $8 a month. Why they matter: they replace the agency quote for a first version.

4. A frontier AI model as your assistant

As of mid-October 2026, Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 holds the top spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 58, ahead of Meta's Muse Wonder Full (56) and GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon (both 53). For volume work the cheaper tiers do the job: Sonnet 5.5 is $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens, and Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 and $0.50. Why: one subscription replaces a copywriter, an analyst and a junior developer for first drafts. You still check the work.

5. Video without a studio

AI video tools now cost roughly $0.08 to $0.15 per second at the cheap end. Google's Gemini Omni Flash is about $0.10 a second with audio, and Alibaba's Wan 3.0 is about $0.083. A 10-second clip is under a dollar or two, though most usable shots take 2 to 5 tries. Why: it replaces a stock-footage subscription and a basic edit for short social content. See our full video rank on the homepage.

6. Music without a licensing middleman

Google Flow Music costs about one cent per song on its $8 plan. Be careful with licensing: its pricing page does not state commercial use, and its output carries a watermark. ElevenLabs Music starts at $6 a month and covers commercial use, but only for around 11 to 16 songs. Why: it replaces stock-music subscriptions, as long as you check the license for what you plan to do. See the music rank on the homepage.

7. Notes you own

Obsidian is free and keeps your notes as plain files on your own device. Sync costs $4 a month if billed annually. Why: your notes and research are the raw material for everything above, and nobody should be able to lock you out of them. Read our Obsidian review.

Cut a middleman only when the replacement is cheaper and you can still check the result.

The honest caveat

Cutting the middleman also cuts what they did for you: support, quality control, legal cover and distribution. A marketplace sends you buyers. An agency is accountable. AI output needs checking, and music and video licenses vary. The tools above are must-haves because they are cheap, replaceable, and keep you in control. They are not a promise of income.
