The AI Orchestra
Method

Claude, Gemini, GPT, Grok, Kimi — each AI has specific strengths. Using all of them as a coordinated team is how you build faster, smarter, and better than using any one tool alone.

// BY WEBSITEMOB// MAY 2026// 6 MIN READ

Most people pick one AI and use it for everything. That's like hiring one person and expecting them to be the architect, the builder, the designer, the copywriter, and the accountant. Every AI model has strengths and weaknesses. The secret is knowing which AI to use for which task — and building a workflow where they hand off to each other.

This is the AI Orchestra Method. Each model has a role. The output of one feeds into the next. The result is faster, higher quality, and more reliable than any single model could produce alone.

You're not a coder.
You're a conductor.
The AIs are your orchestra.

The Orchestra

STRATEGY + UX
Claude
// Strategy, UX, Architecture, Writing
Claude excels at nuanced reasoning, user experience decisions, long-form writing, and balanced strategic thinking. When you need to think through a problem carefully — what should this app do, who is it for, what's the right approach — Claude is where you start. Also the best model for editing, copywriting, and anything requiring judgment.
BEST FOR: Planning, UX review, content, complex reasoning, code review
RESEARCH
Kimi
// Research, Tool Discovery, Deep Reading
Kimi (by Moonshot AI) has an enormous context window and excels at reading and synthesizing large documents, finding tools and resources, and deep research tasks. When you need to understand an entire codebase, read a long PDF, or research a topic comprehensively, Kimi handles volumes that other models struggle with.
BEST FOR: Research, reading long documents, discovering tools, competitor analysis
BUILD
Gemini
// Complex Builds, Execution, Multi-File Code
Gemini (especially Gemini 2.5 Pro) is the strongest model for complex coding tasks — multi-file projects, intricate logic, full-stack generation. When you have a clear spec and need it built, Gemini executes. Warning: it tends to over-engineer. Use Claude's output as the spec, then have Gemini build it. GPT to clean up what Gemini produces.
BEST FOR: Complex builds, large codebases, execution from a clear spec
CLEANUP
ChatGPT (GPT-4o)
// Cleanup, Simplification, Polish
GPT is excellent at simplifying complex code, fixing bugs, improving readability, and general cleanup tasks. After Gemini builds something large and complex, GPT strips it down to what it needs to be. Also good for quick iterations, debugging, and explaining what code does in plain English.
BEST FOR: Code cleanup, debugging, simplification, explanation
CREATIVE
Grok
// Creative Concepts, Brainstorming, Lateral Thinking
Grok (by xAI) is the most creative and unconventional of the major models. It's less reliable for technical tasks, but for brainstorming names, generating unusual concepts, coming up with marketing angles, and pushing ideas in directions other AIs won't go — Grok is uniquely useful. Use it early in ideation, not late in execution.
BEST FOR: Brainstorming, naming, marketing concepts, creative briefs

Step 1 — Claude defines the strategy. What are we building? Who is it for? What problem does it solve? What's the UX philosophy? Claude produces a clear brief that every other AI can work from. This is the most important step — a bad brief produces bad results from every model downstream.

Step 2 — Kimi does the research. What tools exist? What does the competition look like? Are there open-source libraries that do part of what we need? Kimi reads widely and synthesizes quickly. Feed it relevant documents, competitor sites, or technical specs.

Step 3 — Gemini builds. With Claude's spec and Kimi's research, Gemini has everything it needs to execute. Give it the full brief and tell it to build. Expect a working first version. Expect it to be slightly over-engineered. That's fine — that's what step 4 is for.

Step 4 — GPT cleans up. Take Gemini's output and ask GPT to simplify, remove unnecessary complexity, fix bugs, and improve readability. GPT is meticulous about cleanup in a way that Gemini isn't.

Step 5 — Claude reviews. Final pass. Claude checks the result against the original brief. Does it match the UX intent? Is anything confusing? Does the copy need editing? Claude is the quality gate at the end.

When to Break the Pattern

The workflow above is the default, not a rigid rule. For quick tasks — a single component, a short article, a simple bug fix — Claude alone is usually enough. For early-stage creative work, put Grok before Claude to generate unusual ideas before you settle on a direction. For research-heavy projects, Kimi might run in parallel with Claude rather than sequentially.

The orchestra metaphor holds: a conductor doesn't use every instrument in every piece. You learn which instruments each passage needs.

The spec is the most

Practical Tips

Context bridges are critical. When handing off between models, paste the previous model's output into the next conversation. Each AI starts fresh — it doesn't remember what the last one said. The context bridge is what keeps the orchestra playing the same song.

Save your prompts. When you find a prompt that works well for a specific model, save it. Build a personal library of prompts for each model's specialty. This compounds over time — your orchestra gets better with every project.

Use the right model for the right task, not the newest model for everything. The latest Gemini isn't necessarily better than Claude for UX decisions. The newest GPT isn't necessarily better than Kimi for research. Model selection is a skill. The orchestra metaphor holds: a conductor doesn't use every instrument in every piece.
