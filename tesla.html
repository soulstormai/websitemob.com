Dispute Log

Timeline

  1. Jul 28
    My account

    Routine Tesla Service visit. The only finding was normal wear. No battery fault was reported.

  2. Aug 6
    Record

    The 8 year / 100,000 mile battery warranty ends.

  3. Week of Sep 10
    My account

    I drove over a road divider on a flooded road. I saw no warnings on the dash and charging stayed normal afterward. The car's Service Mode alert log, which I pulled later, shows alerts for coolant valve calibration (see Error codes below).

  4. Sep 10–22
    Record

    The Service Mode alert log lists coolant valve, brake sensor, tire pressure, battery coolant sensor, blower and cell imbalance alerts across these dates. The full list is in the Error codes section.

  5. Sep 21–22
    My account

    I installed a pending software update on Sep 21. Range started dropping on Sep 22, when the dash read 170 miles against the 202 it had always shown.

  6. Sep 23
    My account

    Warnings appeared (BMS_a074 and BMS_a079) and I dropped the car at the Tesla service center. The advisor said the high-voltage battery had to be replaced and quoted about $14,000, or about $11,000 refurbished through Tesla Insurance. The diagnostic findings were not shared with me. A $132 diagnostic fee was charged. The service center would not charge the car or let me charge it, although the Tesla app showed charging as available.

    An insurance claim was opened at drop-off and put on hold pending inspection. I don't recall submitting it.

  7. Sep 23
    Record

    Tesla Insurance logged a loss report. Its file describes the incident differently from how I remember it, including the date of loss and what was hit.

  8. Sep 25–28
    Record

    I emailed the insurer asking for an underbody inspection, the vehicle logs, and the cause in writing. On Sep 28 the adjuster replied that Tesla had told them the battery issue was unrelated to the impact, and that no one would be sent to inspect the battery.

    I also sent Tesla a 12-point written request for the cause, a battery engineer's review, and the software history.

  9. Sep 27
    Record

    I opened a warranty inquiry with NCDS, the dispute program Tesla points owners to. On Oct 5 I sent them the error codes and both quotes.

  10. Sep 28
    My account

    I declined the replacement and had the car towed home at my own expense. The advisor told me the failure was normal wear and chargers, that there were signs of collision damage but not enough to prove it caused the failure, and that none of this would be put in writing. The car had been at the service center since Sep 23. When I picked it up, the dash read 130 miles and 1% charge, and it did not charge past 7%.

  11. Sep 30
    Record

    Tesla Insurance said its coverage investigation was finished, noting the shop reported I was declining work. The car still would not charge past 7%.

  12. Oct 2
    My account

    I installed a newer software update. The car charged normally, up to 74% on a Level 1 charger.

  13. Oct 3
    RecordMy account

    I asked Tesla Insurance to withdraw the claim and restore my premium. I sent Tesla's support and press contacts a timeline asking for a refund of the $132 diagnostic fee and the written diagnostic report. That evening BMS_a074 had cleared after the second software update, but BMS_a079 was still showing, the car showed "Charge Complete" as soon as I plugged in, and the screen alerted me to replace the battery pack with the charge at 23%.

  14. Oct 6
    Record

    Tesla Insurance confirmed it would close the claim with no further action. It also said the incident stays on my policy as an at-fault single-vehicle incident because it was reported, even though I withdrew it and nothing was paid. It said the premium is handled by a separate department. When I called that department I was sent back to the adjuster.

  15. Oct 7
    My account

    I opened a dispute with LexisNexis over how the incident is recorded in my claims history. Tesla Insurance keeps leaving me on hold when I ask for escalations to remove the claim.

    The car still displays the battery error. If I perform a reset before charging, it charges to 80% and to 100%.

Trying to get help

My account

From Sep 23 to Oct 6, 13 days, I was passed between Tesla Service, Warranty and Insurance, including between my own adjuster and the policy services department.

  • No single person owned my case at any point.
  • Hold times were long.
  • Callbacks and escalations I was promised did not come.

What I'm asking for

  • A written explanation of the diagnosis, including what BMS_a079 means and what triggers it, and the diagnostic report.
  • An independent evaluation of the high-voltage battery, with the vehicle logs reviewed by a battery specialist.
  • A refund of the $132 diagnostic fee.
  • Correction or removal of the incident entry, and my premium restored to its earlier rate.
  • If the failure is not found to come from the impact, a goodwill repair, since the first logged alert came 35 days after warranty ended.

Questions I can't answer

  1. The car charged normally after a software update and then showed a fault again. Does that point to software, to the battery, or to something else?
  2. Should a claim opened by a shop at drop-off, then put on hold and withdrawn with no payout, count as an at-fault accident?
  3. The alert log shows cooling, brake and tire alerts starting the day of the impact, but no high-voltage isolation, pack breach or coolant leak alerts. Does that pattern point to physical damage or to an aging battery? A log alone can't say, and an inspector would need to look underneath.
  4. Has anyone had a Model 3 battery fail just after warranty and gotten a repair or credit? How did you get it?
  5. Has anyone successfully had an insurance incident entry corrected? Who did you go to?

My view

Opinion, not a finding

The same company diagnosed the car, quoted the repair, and supplied the findings the insurer relied on. I think that is a conflict of interest, and I would like an independent party to look at the battery.

The refurbished-battery price was lower only if I carried Tesla Insurance, and I felt pressure to trade the car in. That is a lot of pressure to put on an owner who has not been shown the diagnosis.

I also think a premium shouldn't double over a claim that paid nothing and that the shop's own findings did not support.

For context: in 2019 Tesla's CEO said publicly that Model 3 battery modules should last 300,000 to 500,000 miles. That was a public statement, not a warranty term, and I'm not treating it as one.
